Turning the console on, opening it and signing in: [Turn on the web console and HTTP](/simlock/docs/worker/console-and-http). Sign-in through your company's proxy: [Sign people in through a sign-in proxy](/simlock/docs/gateway/sign-in-proxy).

A worker's console shows that worker. A gateway's shows every worker that joined, every lease on them and the fleet queue; what a gateway adds is under [On a gateway](#on-a-gateway). A worker that joined a gateway has no console of its own.

## Actions

The console mostly shows. With an operator token (or proxy sign-in), it also offers these changes; each asks to confirm when it is destructive. With a `viewer` token, the sidebar shows **Read-only** and all mutating actions are hidden.

| Action | Where | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Release** | A lease's pane, a device's Holder card, the search | Releases the lease. The holder's next command on the device fails. |
| **Cancel request** | A waiting request, the search | The requester stops waiting and gets an error. |
| **Drain**, **Undrain** | A worker's row and page, on a gateway | As `simlock worker drain` and `undrain`. A drain offers **Undo**. |
| **Update…**, **Update now** | A worker's page and the search, on a gateway | Updates the worker. See [Update a worker](#update-a-worker). |
| **Remove worker** | A disconnected worker, on a gateway | Forgets the worker, after you type its name. It can join again with a worker token. |

Everything else (saving baselines, settings, tokens) is done on the command line.

## Find your way

The sidebar leads to every page, in groups: **Overview** and **Inbox**; **Operate**: **Fleet**, **Leases**, **Queue** and **Baselines**; **Observe**: **Screens**, **Activity** and **Usage**; and **Settings**. **Inbox** shows how many items need you. At the bottom, the account box shows the connection, who you are signed in as, and a **Sign out** icon; hover over the name to see it in full. On a gateway behind the latest release, a line above the account reads, for example, `simlock 0.3.0 · 0.4.0 available`. The button at the foot of the sidebar collapses or expands it; on a phone, open its menu above the page.

- Every page has its own address, such as `/leases`, so you can bookmark, reload or share it.
- The search (⌘K) finds pages, workers, devices, leases (by label too) and requests, and offers their actions.
- On **Queue**, **Inbox** and **Activity**, the item you select opens in a panel over the list. On **Leases** it opens in a pane beside the table. On **Fleet**, a worker opens as its own page, and a device opens its streaming page.
- Lists show 25 rows at a time, with a pager for 50 or 100.
- The console follows your system's light or dark setting, unless you pick one in [Settings](#settings).
- It is laid out for a 1440 pixel wide window, where each page fits without scrolling. Narrower, the Overview's right-hand cards move under its chart. Below 1280 pixels a lease opens as a panel over the list. Below 768 the sidebar becomes a bar at the top with a menu button, and tables stack into blocks (Fleet and the worker's tables below 1100). No page scrolls sideways, down to a 360 pixel wide phone.

Pages update by themselves: a new lease or a booting device shows within about a second, readings such as CPU load within 15 seconds. The bottom of the sidebar shows the connection:

| It says | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Connected | Up to date. |
| Reconnecting — data from 12 s ago | The console lost the daemon. What you see is as old as it says. It keeps trying and catches up by itself. |
| Daemon is starting | The daemon is back and still starting. Devices, capacity and the warm pool appear once startup finishes. |

## Overview

The console opens here.

- **Range**: **1 h**, **6 h**, **24 h** or **7 d**, at the top right. It sets the figures and charts below.
- **Banner**: when something needs you, a line counts the problems from **Inbox**, links the first and says how many more there are. **Open Inbox** goes to the list. With nothing to do, there is no banner.
- **Tiles**, each opening its page: **Leased** (leases held, of the devices running) and **Ready** (devices ready to lease) open **Leases** and **Fleet**; **Waiting**, with the longest wait, opens **Queue**; **Wait p95** for the range (how long the slowest 1 in 20 requests waited) opens **Usage** on the same range; **Ending soon** (leases with under 5 minutes left) opens **Leases**; **Workers** connected opens **Fleet**. A figure the daemon could not read shows a dash, never zero.
- **Fleet**: the Fleet table for up to eight workers, those that need attention first. A row opens the worker. **Open fleet** shows all.
- **Leases held**: a chart over the range, with the current and the highest figure. A table of the numbers is there for screen readers.
- **Capacity**: per platform, how much of the room your workers may run is leased.
- **Queue**: the three longest waiting requests. **Ending soon**: the two leases that end first. Each row opens its request or lease. A queue that cannot be read says so instead of showing empty.
- **Requests waiting**: a bar for each period of the range. **Device slots in use** is on **Usage**.

## Inbox

Everything that needs you. Each row is the condition and the worker or device it is about. An item leaves the list as soon as the daemon reports it cleared, and its page then says "Resolved." Select an item to see the facts that matter for it, such as the RAM used of the budget, and the last hour's events about that device or worker.

| Item | Means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `over RAM budget` | The worker's devices use more RAM than its budget. `simlock status` shows `(over limit)`. | Release leases, or lower the device limits. See [Run more devices at once](/simlock/docs/worker/capacity). |
| `quarantined` | Simlock could not clean up a device. **Cause** says what failed: `Purge failed`, `Shutdown failed`, `Delete failed`, or `Stalled while provisioning` or `reclaiming`. Simlock retries in the background and leases the device to no one meanwhile. | Wait for the retry, or see [A worker misbehaves](/simlock/docs/troubleshooting/worker). |
| `stalled` | A device has been `provisioning` or `reclaiming` longer than it should, with nothing working on it. `simlock doctor` reports the same devices. | See [A worker misbehaves](/simlock/docs/troubleshooting/worker). |

A gateway adds worker items; see [On a gateway](#inbox-on-a-gateway).

## Fleet

The workers the daemon knows. On a worker that is one row: this machine. **Add worker** links to [Add a worker to a gateway](/simlock/docs/gateway/add-a-worker).

**All**, **Issues** and **Busy** narrow the list; the search box beside them matches a worker's name, id or system. **Issues** shows workers with something wrong, **Busy** workers using three quarters of their device slots or more.

A worker with something wrong sits under **Needs attention**, on a tinted row; the rest under **Healthy**. A row has the name and system, and a word and a colour for the worst thing wrong. Hover over or focus the word for why, such as how long a lost worker has been gone or how full its disk is.

| Colour | When |
| --- | --- |
| Red | Health is `failed`, memory pressure is `critical`, `new devices paused`, or CPU, memory or disk is 95% full or more. |
| Amber | `starting` or `over RAM budget`, memory pressure `warn`, `warming paused`, or a figure 85% full or more. |
| Green | Nothing is wrong. The word is `connected`. |

Every row shows the iOS and Android slots as a meter with the leased/total fraction, CPU, memory and disk with a bar each, and the leases held. Slot counts are read out to screen readers. Unavailable load shows `—`, never zero. Each cell is one line; a long value is cut with an ellipsis and shows in full on hover. **⋯** holds the row's actions. Under both groups, **Worker events** lists the workers' events of the last hour, newest at the top.

### A worker's page

Select a worker's row. The title reads **Fleet / name**, with the worker's status beside it. At the top right are its first action, such as **Drain**, and **⋯** for the rest. Four rings show CPU, memory, disk and slots, each with its figure and a line of context.

The left card:

- **Slots**: iOS and Android slots, leased over total: a square per slot up to ten, a meter above ten.
- **Host**: the OS, each platform tool's version, the Simlock version, the devices kept warm and the worker's id.
- **Capabilities**: each optional module, such as screen streaming, as `on`, `off`, or `unavailable` with its reason.
- **Baselines here**: the baselines saved on this worker, with their sizes.

The right card has two tabs:

- **Devices**: every device with its model, runtime and state. A leased device shows its holder, the time left and when it was granted; a ready device when it booted. **Watch** opens its screen.
- **Warm pool**: whether the worker keeps devices warm, the running slots it holds back, and per target and learned kind its origin (`configured` or `learned`), how many it wants, how many are ready and booting. A target it cannot fill shows why, such as `runtime-missing`. Under it, **Installs in progress** lists runtimes and images downloading. See [Make leases start faster](/simlock/docs/worker/warm-pool).

Under the tabs, **Events on this worker** lists its events, newest first; older ones load as you scroll.

### A device

Select a device to open its drawer: model, runtime, state, the baseline and version it was copied from (if any), holder, lease, UDID, and **Watch**.

**Watch** opens the device's streaming page at `/fleet/<worker>/devices/<device>/`, so a pasted link or a reload opens it too. The live screen plays in a phone-shaped frame, leased or not. Two pills over it show `LIVE` with the frame rate, and the codec, picture size and bandwidth. Beside it: the **Holder** card (the agent, the lease id, the time left and last renewal, with **Copy lease id**, **Lease details** and **Release**), the device's facts and its recent events. A device with no lease has no Holder card. The page only watches: it sends no taps or keys.

- **Stop watching** closes the stream and returns to the worker; leaving the page any other way closes it too.
- A device that isn't running says why, such as "The device is shut down. Its screen shows while it runs.", and the picture starts once it is `ready` again.
- With streaming off, the page says "Streaming is off on" and the worker's name. See [Watch a device's screen](/simlock/docs/using-devices/watch-the-screen).
- An older link ending in `?drawer=screen` opens the streaming page.

### Update a worker

On a gateway only. A worker behind the newest version shows `simlock 0.3.0 · 0.4.0 available` under its name. Its page's **⋯** menu and the search offer **Update…**:

- The dialog says how many leases the worker holds. With none it installs at once; otherwise it takes no new leases, waits for them to end, and restarts.
- **Update** shows a toast: `Updating <name> to <version>`, `<name> is already updating to <version>`, or `<name> already runs <version>`.
- While it runs, the worker's state reads `updating` and its card shows the stage: `waiting for 2 leases`, `installing` or `restarting`.
- While it waits for leases, **Update now** asks `Install <version> on <name> now?`; **Install now** restarts it at once, and its leases keep running through the restart. A disconnected worker offers no **Update now**.
- An `incompatible` worker offers **Update…** whatever the latest version.
- A failed update shows a red banner, `Update to 0.4.0 failed (installing): <message>`, with **Update again**, and the Inbox lists it. The worker runs its old version.

## Leases

Every lease on the worker, as `simlock list --leases` shows.

- Each row: the holder (**Held by**), with the lease's label above it when it has one; the device and runtime; the worker; and the time left (**Expires in**) as a countdown with a bar. Under 5 minutes left turns amber.
- **Ending < 5 min**, **iOS** and **Android** narrow the list; the box beside them searches holder, label, device and worker. The search (⌘K) shows a lease as `<label> · <holder> · <device>`.
- The holder is the identity that took the lease. Over HTTP, that is the token's label: give each agent's token one. From the CLI or MCP server on the machine, it is the agent identity.
- A long holder is shortened in the middle; hover over it for the whole requester identity. A chip names a known agent tool, or shows a dot.

Beside the table, a pane summarises every lease, whatever the filter leaves: **By holder**, **By platform** (iOS and Android slots leased), **Ending in 5 min** and **Longest held**.

Select a lease and the pane shows it instead: the holder, a ring and countdown against its original time, **Watch**, **Copy id** and **Release**, the device, worker, grant time, token, lease id, device id, the baseline (if made from one), and **Recent events** of the last hour. **Watch** shows only while the worker streams the device. **Copy id** copies the whole id; if the browser can't copy, the id stays on screen to select. A lease that ended says "This lease is not held." Close the pane to see the summary again.

## Queue

Every request waiting for a device, as `simlock list --requests` shows. Above the list, **Wait p95** for the last hour.

Each row: the request's place in its queue, who asked, how long it has waited, the device asked for, and its stage: `queued` while it waits its turn, `starting` while the daemon finds, creates, boots or downloads a device for it. A request leaves the list once it gets a device, fails or is cancelled.

Select a request for when it was made, its place, its stage, **Cancel request**, and for a `queued` one why it isn't placed yet:

| Why | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Behind an earlier request | Another request for the same kind of device came first. |
| Full: device limit | The worker has as many devices as it may keep. |
| Full: running limit | The worker runs as many devices as it may. |
| Full: running limit for iOS | The same, for iOS (or Android). |
| Full: RAM budget | Another device would go over the RAM budget. |
| Full: host memory pressure | The host is short of memory and paused new devices. |
| A matching device is starting | A device that fits is booting and will serve it. |
| Every matching device is leased or absent | The request names phones that are all in use or unplugged. |

To run more devices: [Run more devices at once](/simlock/docs/worker/capacity).

## Baselines

Every baseline the worker holds, grouped by platform, each group with its count. Each row: name, the model and runtime it was saved on, size, when it was built, and `ready` or `stale`. Hover or focus `stale` for its reason. **All**, **Stale** and **Ready** narrow the list; the search box finds a name, model, runtime or worker. The counts follow the filter.

**Prepare baseline** opens the guide. The console doesn't save or remove baselines: see [Save a prepared device](/simlock/docs/worker/save-a-prepared-device).

## Screens

Watch agents' devices side by side.

- The table lists running devices across workers: holder, device, worker, state and how long it has been leased. It opens on **Leased**; **Ready** shows devices nobody holds, **All** both.
- **Watch** adds a screen to **Watching** at the top; **Stop** or **Stop watching** removes it. **Open** goes to the device's own page.
- Up to four screens at once; **Watch** is unavailable on other rows until you stop one. Nothing streams until you watch it.
- The address remembers what you watch, so a reload, a shared link or coming back in the same tab shows the same screens.
- Watched screens pause while the tab is hidden, and stop when you leave the page or sign out. A device that stops running leaves the list. A stream that fails shows its reason in its place.

## Activity

Events, newest first, starting with the last hour. New ones appear at the top; older ones load as you scroll. Each row: event name, what it is about, the worker and the time. The search box and the range (**1 h**, **6 h**, **24 h** or **7 d**) are in the header. When the events loaded don't reach back as far as the range, the page says so, and **Load older events** fetches more.

A card on the left filters by **Kind** (**Leases**, **Queue**, **Devices**, **Workers**, **Baselines** or **Other**), by **Problems only** and by **Worker**. Each option shows how many events it would show. Tick several in a group for all of them; none for everything. Events with no worker are under **Other** in the Worker group. A chart above the list counts the events shown per minute over the last hour.

Select an event for its whole payload and a link to its lease, worker, request or Inbox item. What each event means: [Events](/simlock/docs/reference/events).

## Usage

The figures for a range, as `simlock stats` gives them: requests, grants, rejections, wait and hold times, grants by source (warm, booted or provisioned), rejections by reason, device incidents, and figures per platform and per requester. Pick **1 hour**, **6 hours**, **24 hours** or **7 days**.

## Settings

- **Console**: your preferences in this browser: theme (**System**, **Light** or **Dark**), sidebar (**Expanded** or **Collapsed**; a phone's menu ignores it), the default range for **Overview** and **Activity**, rows per page, and holder ids **Short** (cut in the middle) or **Full**. A change applies at once and stays in this browser.
- **Daemon**: the daemon's live configuration by section, secrets masked. Read-only: **Copy** next to a key copies its `simlock config set <key> <value>` command. It needs an operator or viewer token; with another, the card says your token can't read this daemon's settings, and an older daemon that can't report them is said so. On a gateway it shows the gateway's own configuration.

## On a gateway

The gateway's console has the same pages, with the whole fleet.

### Overview on a gateway

- **Workers** shows the connected workers of all the gateway knows, such as `3` `of 4`.
- **Leased** counts leases on every worker. It shows `—` until every worker has reported, and a platform under **Capacity** says how many workers are `not read yet`.

### Fleet on a gateway

| Word | Colour | Means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `disconnected` | Red | The worker's connection closed. The gateway shows what it last reported. |
| `incompatible` | Red | Connected, but its Simlock version can't talk to the gateway's. It gets no requests. |
| `updating` | Amber | Updating; takes no new leases. Hover for `waiting for 2 leases`, `installing` or `restarting`. |
| `drained` | Amber | Takes no new leases from the gateway. Its leases keep running. |
| `starting` | Amber | Starting up. |

- A disconnected worker's row has **View leases** first in **⋯**, a drained one's **Undrain**.
- A disconnected worker's page shows "No reading while disconnected" instead of its load, and its devices and capacity as last reported.
- An incompatible worker's page shows only **Protocol**, with the gateway's and the worker's version ranges, so you can tell which to update.
- The status beside the title is the connection; a banner says `Drained` when drained.
- A disconnected worker stays listed for 24 hours by default (`gateway.disconnectedRetentionMs`), and while it holds a lease made through the gateway.

### Leases and baselines on a gateway

**Leases** lists the leases on every worker, both those made through the gateway and those agents on a worker machine took from it directly. The **Worker** column names the worker; the selected lease's **Worker** field links to it. A lease made through the gateway has an id of the worker's id, a dot, and the worker's own lease id, unless the agent chose the id. **Baselines** adds a **Worker** column.

### Queue on a gateway

A request's **Queue** field reads `the gateway's queue` while the gateway looks for a worker. Under it, each worker and why it didn't take the request:

| It says | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Full, or Full: and a limit | No room under that limit. |
| Busy with its own queue | Requests wait in the worker's own queue, such as from agents on that machine. |
| Not chosen this time | It could take it, but the gateway gave it to another request first. |
| Drained | The worker is drained. |
| Not connected | The worker is disconnected. |
| Protocol mismatch | The worker is incompatible. |
| Not ready yet | The worker hasn't finished reporting its devices since it connected. |
| Not running | The worker's daemon isn't healthy. |
| Model not offered | It doesn't offer the model or class asked for. |
| OS not installed | It offers the model, with no matching OS version installed. |
| No iOS support, No Android support | It doesn't support the platform. |
| Mode not served | The request, or its baseline, names an iOS mode the worker's `ios.modes` doesn't list. |
| Missing capability | It lacks an optional module the request needs. |
| Cannot keep these slim categories | The request's `except` categories aren't all slimmed there, or it wouldn't give a slim device. |

How the gateway picks a worker: [Workers and gateways](/simlock/docs/concepts/workers-and-gateways).

### Inbox on a gateway

| Item | Colour | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `disconnected` | Red | Check the worker machine and `simlock daemon logs`. When it is gone for good, remove it. |
| `incompatible` | Red | Update the older of the worker and the gateway. |
| `drained` | Amber | **Undrain** once its maintenance is done. |
| `update failed` | Amber | **Update…** to try again, or read **From**, **To**, **Stage** and **When** first. The worker runs its old version. |

A worker both disconnected and drained shows as two items, since each clears on its own.

### Usage on a gateway

**Usage** adds **Per worker**: each worker's requests, grants, rejections and 95th-percentile wait in the range. For a message when something goes wrong: [Send alerts](/simlock/docs/gateway/alerts).
